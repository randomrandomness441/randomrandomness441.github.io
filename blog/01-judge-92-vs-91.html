FUNDA LOG · 01 / 07 from building a funda-question generator

My LLM judge scored 92.3%. Counting characters scored 91.5%.

A rule with no model in it matched the labels almost as well as the judge did. The judge was worth 0.8 points over a character counter.

A rule with no model in it, "call a question constructed if it's at least 140 characters long", matched the labels 91.5% of the time. My LLM judge matched them 92.3% of the time. Same bank, about 3,400 questions. The judge was worth 0.8 points over a character counter.

Some context. I'm building something that writes quiz questions in the style the Indian quiz circuit calls "funda": questions you can reason your way to, as opposed to trivia you either know or don't. To train it I needed to know which of my questions were funda. So I wrote a judge. One prompt to glm-4.5-air, ten questions per call: does this question hide its answer behind indirect clues, misdirection or wordplay, or does it just ask for a fact?

The labels it was scored against came from where each question lived. 1,858 questions from funda quiz decks, 1,531 from an OpenTDB trivia dump. The judge called 88.4% of the first group constructed and 3.0% of the second. That works out to 92.3%. I put it into the pipeline on July 27, labelled the whole bank with it, and built the generator's training corpus from the rows it approved.

◆ to fill in before publishingwhat made you go back and run something dumb as a baseline, and what you felt when the 91.5 came up.

The baseline went into my notes on August 10. One rule, no prompt, no API bill:

constructed if len >= 140 characters      91.5%
the construction judge                    92.3%

It wins because my two piles differ in length more than in anything else. Funda questions in the bank have a median of 422 characters. Trivia has 64. Any length detector wins on that. I'd also capped stored questions at 300 characters, which made it worse: most funda questions got cut off mid-prose with the question removed, while a 64-character trivia question came through whole.

I ran the obvious follow-up. Take 50 trivia questions, pad each one with a paragraph of clue prose, and re-judge.

judged constructed (300-char cap)uncapped
original trivia, ~70 chars4%4%
same trivia, padded to ~35376%58%
real funda, ~40898%88%

283 extra characters, 72 extra points. The obvious reading is that the judge reads length.

Then I looked at where the padding came from. I'd generated it with the tool I use to write clue chains, and that tool's prompt asks for clues "that let a solver infer the answer." So a chunk of that jump may be real. Pad a recall question with inferable clues and it stops being pure recall. The 4-to-76 table is confounded, and I only saw that once a later judge's derivations started quoting the padding back at me. What survives without it is the baseline, which has no padding in it at all, and one other thing: I pulled questions out of a quizmaster's recorded session, a set that never touched my bank, and the only one the judge rejected was 137 characters long.

The corpus damage doesn't depend on any of that. The judge keeps long questions and drops short ones. The quizmaster's own example, "Which 2017 Hollywood film was shipped to cinemas under the codename Pound Foolish?" (answer: It, via Pennywise), is 75 characters. My pipeline would have labelled it trivia and thrown it away.

What replaced the judge asks a different question. Given the question and the answer, does the answer explain the question's details, or just fit them? "Groundlings paid a penny... the money was gathered in a particular manner and kept safe. What term originated from this?" The money went into a box, the room holding the box became the box office. The answer explains the setup. "Known for his distinctive red helmet... who won the 2018 Monaco Grand Prix?" Ricciardo fits the description. He doesn't explain it. The model also has to write out the derivation, and the code refuses to count an EXPLAINS verdict that arrives with an empty one. I'd earlier seen the prompt say "explained" with no steps on 67 to 79% of items. A guard the model applies to itself isn't a guard.

On 42 questions from the quizmaster's recorded sessions (39 scored), 32 came back EXPLAINS, 82%. All seven misses are questions where the pivot is a picture on the slide, which the judge never sees. The 15 I counted as text-only passed 15 for 15. Unpadded trivia: 0 of 25 in one set, 1 of 67 in another.

Then I padded trivia again, properly this time. Neutral facts only (where it is, what category, when it was founded), and lengths drawn from the real funda length distribution so the padded set covers the same range. 1,560 items scored, 5.5% called EXPLAINS. Under 100 characters, 0%. Over 300, 6.6%. That's a slope, but it's nothing like a cliff.

I read the 86 that got through. They weren't a length effect either. The padding had handed over the derivation. One of them reads "The first derives from a verb meaning to think. The second is related to a noun meaning mind or desire," before asking what Odin's ravens Huginn and Muninn are named for. The judge read that correctly. My control leaked.

If your two classes differ in length, run a character counter first. Mine is one line, and it reached my notes two weeks after the judge did.
