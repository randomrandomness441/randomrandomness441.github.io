FUNDA LOG · 03 / 07 from building a funda-question generator

The two classes differed by 0.42. The feature I built from it differed by 0.017.

I had a good signal and I computed it into a bad one.

For every quiz question I measured how often a small closed-book model could guess the answer. Two versions: from the full question (P_full), and from a stripped-down rewrite of it (P_direct). Then I did what felt like analysis and derived features. clue_lift is P_full − P_direct, how much the clues help. reducibility is P_direct / max(P_full, 0.001), how much of the question you can throw away. prior_lift is P_full − P_bare, where P_bare is the model's hit rate with no question at all.

I fit it on 962 labelled questions, 500 from the funda bank and 462 from an OpenTDB trivia dump. These are the effect sizes (Cohen's d, where above 0.8 is large and below 0.2 is negligible):

The two raw measurements carry a huge effect. Trivia is much easier to guess than funda, which is the whole premise. The features I built from them carry almost none.

Look at why subtraction failed, because the arithmetic is small enough to do by hand. Funda drops from 0.200 to 0.094 going from the full question to the stripped one, a loss of 0.106. Trivia drops from 0.603 to 0.514, a loss of 0.089. Both classes lose almost the same amount. They differ in level, by about 0.42, and barely at all in gap. Subtracting throws away level and keeps gap. It's the one operation that discards the thing that separates them. The correlation between P_full and P_direct is 0.74, so they were mostly the same number to begin with.

The ratio failed for a different reason. When P_full is near zero, the max(..., 0.001) floor in the denominator multiplies whatever noise P_direct has by up to a thousand. The standard deviations came out at roughly ten times the means. The formula's artifact swamped any signal underneath.

And P_bare is zero for every row in this bank. So prior_lift, which I'd built into the decision tree, is P_full with a longer name.

The fitted rule that came out was one line:

TRIVIA if P_direct >= 0.05 else FUNDA
ruleaccuracy
always say funda52.0%
my hand-set gate (reducibility and lift thresholds)71.3%
P_direct >= 0.0573.2%

I tried every AND, OR and max combination of the signals. Each one scored the same or worse than the single threshold.

A derived feature can't contain information that wasn't in its inputs. It's worth building only if it encodes some real structure, such as removing a confound or putting two scales on the same footing. Neither applied here. I'd taken two good columns and computed two bad ones, and the gate I'd built around them did no better than one threshold.

Two things I should say about the 73.2%. It was fit against OpenTDB trivia, which is short, Western, and plainly worded, so some of that d of 1.09 may be the same length-and-domain separation I wrote about in the first post. And with four samples per question, a good part of the remaining error is sampling noise.
